## 1. Challenge overview

The SEVA First Innovation Challenge is an institution-led innovation challenge for student teams in the participating South Zone States and Union Territories. Its stated focus is on practical solutions to problems of local and national importance, with attention to feasibility, impact, and implementation.

The official South Zone page lists **Andhra Pradesh, Karnataka, Keralam, Tamil Nadu, Telangana, Andaman & Nicobar Islands, Lakshadweep, and Puducherry**.

### APAAR-Verify AI — team assessment of theme fit

**Suggested primary alignment: Theme 4 — Shikshit & Kaushal Bharat.**

Reasoning: APAAR-Verify AI is intended to improve student-data management, academic-record quality, institution workflows and controlled academic verification. Those functions are most directly related to education innovation and digital education infrastructure.

**Possible secondary alignment: Theme 5 — Surakshit Bharat.**

The official theme includes digital public infrastructure. This may be relevant if the challenge organisers classify the proposed student-record and verification system as a public digital infrastructure solution.

> **Important:** This is the team's interpretation of the published theme descriptions, not an official SEVA classification or endorsement. Confirm the exact theme and problem-statement option available in the live portal before submission.

## 3. Official innovation tracks

The official challenge describes two tracks:

### Track A — Product or Technology

A device, prototype, material, process, software system or engineering solution that can be built, demonstrated and deployed.

### Track B — Business Model or Governance

A new delivery model, service design, financing mechanism, community institution, process re-engineering, or governance and regulatory innovation.

Both tracks have equal standing and are evaluated against the same common criteria.

**Suggested APAAR-Verify AI track: Track A — Product or Technology.**

The main deliverable is a software platform and prototype. A governance/adoption model can support the technical solution, but it should not be used to mislabel the primary software product as Track B.

**Official source:** [SEVA — Two Innovation Tracks](https://sevafirst.mic.gov.in/tracks)

## 4. Participation and institution requirements

The official portal describes participation as **institution-led**:

1. The institution nominates a coordinator (for example, a principal, head of department or innovation-cell in-charge).
2. The coordinator registers the institution using the relevant institution details and official email.
3. The coordinator enters student/team details and submits the student solutions.
4. The coordinator can save drafts and submit them before the submission window closes.

The official registration page lists the participating South Zone States and Union Territories, including **Tamil Nadu**. It also specifies the institution identifier by institution type: UDISE for schools, AISHE for colleges/universities, and the relevant institution code for ITIs/polytechnics.

The official participation page places undergraduate students, postgraduate students and research scholars under the **Open** level. For the Open level, the portal describes an expected demonstrated prototype or pilot with a credible deployment pathway supported by cost, beneficiaries, timeline, impact and scalability.

**Official sources:**
- [Institution registration](https://sevafirst.mic.gov.in/register)
- [How to participate and participation levels](https://sevafirst.mic.gov.in/participate)

### Details from the supplied coordinator guide

The SEVA overview/coordinator guide supplied during project preparation additionally states:

- Up to **5 teams per institute** may be created in the portal.
- Each team needs **2–5 students** to be eligible to submit.
- Participant records include name, date of birth, gender, class/section and roll number.
- The guide describes email OTP verification during coordinator registration.
- The guide's team-formation flow generates a team code.

The separate team-details form supplied for this work says that a **college ID card is mandatory for registration**. Confirm the current portal's exact team-size, OTP and document requirements before relying on these details.

## 5. Submission process and document requirements

The supplied 32-page overview includes an Institute Coordinator Guide. Its submission flow is:

1. Register the institute and coordinator.
2. Sign in to the coordinator account.
3. Create a team and add eligible participants.
4. Select the team for submission.
5. Enter the idea identity: title, theme, track and problem statement.
6. Complete the problem and solution sections.
7. Upload the concept note PDF and submit the idea.
8. Track the submission status from the coordinator dashboard.

The supplied coordinator guide states that the **Concept Note PDF is required and limited to 10 MB**. Supporting documents and a video link are described as optional. It also describes a Save Draft action and a Pending Review status after successful submission.

### Application fields in the separate “Details Required” form

The blank form supplied for this application asks for:

| Field | Limit or response type in the supplied form |
|---|---|
| What problem are you solving? | 500 characters |
| Proposed solution | 1,000 words |
| Who is affected, and where does the problem occur? | 300 words |
| What is innovative or different? | 300 words |
| How does the solution work? | Step-by-step explanation |
| What stage is the solution at? | Idea / Prototype / Working Prototype / Pilot / Deployed |
| Additional notes on stage | 300 words |
| Who will benefit and what impact will it create? | 300 words |
| Estimated cost | 300 words |
| Implementation and scalability | 300 words |
| Evidence | Select applicable evidence types and describe evidence (300 words) |
| Potential adopters | 300 words |
| Support needed | 300 words |

> **Limit discrepancy to resolve:** The supplied coordinator guide's illustrated portal flow gives different limits for some fields (it describes the problem field as 500 words and the impact field as 500 words), while the separate “Details Required” form says 500 **characters** for the problem and 300 words for impact. Use the live portal's field labels/counters and the latest official instructions at the time of submission.

### Working submission identity

### Theme and track checks before submission

The current draft's **“Smart Automation”** theme and **“Software”** track labels do not match the five theme names and the Track A / Track B terminology on the official SEVA overview page.

For this software-platform concept, the team's working recommendation is:

- **Primary theme:** Shikshit & Kaushal Bharat (Theme 4), subject to portal confirmation.
- **Primary track:** Track A — Product or Technology.
- **Secondary theme to consider only if the portal/organisers classify it that way:** Surakshit Bharat (Theme 5), because its focus areas include digital public infrastructure.

The draft references **SIH Problem Statement 26263**. That SIH identifier should not be assumed to be a SEVA problem-statement ID. Confirm whether the SEVA portal offers this as a valid problem statement, whether the application must select a listed SEVA problem, or whether the team can submit it as a participant-defined problem.

### Problem being addressed

The prepared submission explains that AICTE-approved institutions may submit student information in different formats and processes, with incomplete or aggregate-only records. This can make it difficult to maintain consistent student-level data, identify duplicate enrolments, validate APAAR ID formats and compare admissions with approved intake and seat categories. Manual consolidation can slow verification, monitoring and reporting.

### Proposed solution in the prepared draft

APAAR-Verify AI is described as a centralized, role-based student data management and verification platform concept for AICTE-approved institutions. The proposed workflow includes:

1. Individual student entry or bulk CSV/Excel submission.
2. Standardised capture of student, institution, programme, academic-year, admission-category, intake and relevant seat-category data.
3. Validation of required fields, APAAR ID format, missing/inconsistent data, duplicate risks and enrolment totals.
4. Comparison of reported admissions with approved intake and applicable seat categories.
5. Explainable anomaly findings for authorised human review.
6. Role-based dashboards, review actions, audit trail and reporting.
7. Limited, consent-aware verification for authorised stakeholders.

### Beneficiaries identified in the draft

- AICTE teams responsible for institutional approval, student-data monitoring and regulatory reporting.
- AICTE-approved institutions and their admission, registrar and data-management teams.
- Students whose academic records may otherwise be incomplete, duplicated or inconsistent.
- Degree-awarding bodies and other authorised verification stakeholders.

### Prototype status and evidence

The prepared SEVA draft selects **Prototype**, not Pilot or Deployed. It describes interface/workflow materials and a project repository as the available evidence.

The draft explicitly does **not** claim:
- A live connection to the national APAAR registry.
- A production connection to AICTE systems.
- An institutional pilot or deployment.
- Measured reductions in processing time, error rates or fraud.
- Independent performance testing or a formal user survey.

The current project materials should be demonstrated with synthetic/sample data unless the team has explicit authorisation to use real student records.

### Cost estimate in the prepared draft

The submitted planning estimate is **₹50,000–₹75,000** for a first-year pilot at approximately 1–3 institutions. It is a team estimate based on stated assumptions, not a vendor quotation or approved budget. It excludes team salaries, official integration charges and large-scale rollout. Revalidate the assumptions before presenting it as a current estimate.

### Implementation approach in the prepared draft

The draft proposes a controlled pilot with 1–3 AICTE-approved institutions, agreement on a data dictionary and intake references, standard CSV/Excel uploads, authorised data, role definitions, retention rules, exception review, security/privacy assessment and measurable pilot outcomes. Secure APIs and formal APAAR/AICTE integrations are positioned as later steps requiring approvals and agreements.

## 7. Evaluation criteria and preparation checklist

The official Tracks page lists six common evaluation criteria:

1. **Problem clarity** — Is the problem observed first-hand, precisely defined and worth solving?
2. **Originality** — What is genuinely new compared with existing approaches?
3. **Demonstrated feasibility** — Has the solution been built, tested or piloted with evidence?
4. **Cost** — What does one unit or delivery cycle cost, and who pays?
5. **Beneficiary impact** — Who benefits, how many, and by how much?
6. **Scalability** — Can the solution be replicated elsewhere or scaled efficiently?
